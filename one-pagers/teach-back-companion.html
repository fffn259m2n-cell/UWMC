Teach-Back Companion and Instruction Fidelity Evaluator

EHR assistants now explain records to patients. This prototype targets the next two questions: did the patient understand? and does the simplified or translated instruction still say the same thing?

Teach-back (patient side)

  • Each discharge instruction is shown verbatim beside a plain version.
  • The patient explains it in their own words. The tool shows what they covered, what is missing, and possible misunderstandings ("I can stop the blood thinner when I feel better"), always quoting the original and pointing to the care team.
  • Emergency, crisis and medication-change messages are screened first and sent to human help.

Fidelity evaluator (care-team side)

  • Checks any rewrite (human, AI or translated) against the original for doses, frequencies, tablet counts, limits, "do not" statements, 911 instructions, medicine names and reading level.
  • Benchmark: {{BENCH}}

Why it may matter

  • Teach-back is recommended (AHRQ Health Literacy Toolkit, Tool 5) but time-consuming.
  • A 2026 randomised trial in internal medicine found physician-reviewed, AI-generated patient discharge summaries increased patient activation (Rust et al., Lancet Digit Health 2026;8(6):100991). The fidelity evaluator is a candidate aid for that review step.

Status and boundaries

  • Measured on 360 new cases from an independent reviewer, before tuning: flagged 91% of dangerous teach-back explanations; 98% of emergency messages and 60% of crisis messages received 911/988 guidance in the reply. A 911/988 strip is on every screen. Not a safety net; an aid for clinician-led teach-back.
  • {{TBC_TESTS}} automated tests; browser and WCAG automated checks pass; four independent measurement rounds.
  • Fictional patients; draft education text not yet clinician-reviewed; rule-based, not clinically validated.
  • No EHR connection, no PHI, nothing typed is stored.
